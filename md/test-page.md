# Analyze the importance of Ashokan inscriptions for reconstructing Mauryan history. (Answer in 150 words)

Ashokan inscriptions are the most crucial and authentic primary sources for reconstructing Mauryan history, offering direct insight into the period without the later interpolations found in literary texts.

Carved on rocks, pillars, and cave walls across the subcontinent, they establish the vast geographical extent of Ashoka’s empire, from Kandahar in the northwest to Karnataka in the south. They uniquely document his administrative structure, mentioning key officials like _Mahamatras_ and _Rajukas_, and his welfare-oriented state policies.

Crucially, the edicts track Ashoka’s personal ideological shift following the Kalinga War. They detail his adoption and propagation of _Dhamma_—a moral code of social responsibility and religious tolerance—rather than aggressive military expansion. Furthermore, the use of multiple scripts (Brahmi, Kharoshthi, Aramaic, and Greek) highlights the empire's immense cultural and linguistic diversity, confirming the Mauryan state's sophisticated approach to communicating directly with a heterogeneous population.

# Discuss the impact of Macaulay's minute on colonial administration and education in India. (150 Words)

Thomas Macaulay’s 1835 Minute on Education fundamentally transformed both the educational landscape and the administrative structure of colonial India by officially cementing English as the medium of instruction.

-   **Educational Shift:** It abruptly redirected state funding away from Oriental learning (Sanskrit and Arabic) toward Western sciences and literature. It implemented the "Downward Filtration Theory," aiming to educate only a small upper-class Indian elite who would theoretically pass knowledge down to the masses.
    
-   **Administrative Utility:** The immediate administrative intent was practical and economic. The British needed a steady supply of English-speaking Indians to staff the lower ranks of the rapidly expanding colonial bureaucracy and judiciary much cheaper than importing British clerks.
    
-   **Unintended Consequences:** While it successfully created a loyal clerical class—intended to be "Indian in blood and colour, but English in taste"—it inadvertently provided a common _lingua franca_. This enabled leaders from diverse linguistic regions to communicate, absorbing Western concepts of democracy and liberty that ultimately fueled the Indian nationalist movement.
